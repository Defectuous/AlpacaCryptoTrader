# LLM-gated momentum chase — 2026-10-15

**Question:** if the local Ollama model (Underdog-Saluki-27B IQ2, at 192.168.1.151) approves or vetoes every
entry, does the momentum chase work over the last 30 / 60 / 90 days?

**Setup:** same rules as [momentum-chase-2026-10-13](momentum-chase-2026-10-13.md), **long-only** (Alpaca can't
short). 33 coins, hourly bars to 2026-10-15 05:00 UTC, $500 start, 0.25% fee + 0.05% slippage per side.
Two rule sets, chosen on the data *before* the 90-day window. Both pyramid (+25% equity per +2%, max 4) and exit
on the first hourly close against the position:
- **strict**: 3h momentum ≥ 8% (best long-only config Sep 2025 → Jul 2026)
- **loose**: 6h momentum ≥ 5% (many more signals, so the model has more to filter)

Before each entry the model gets the last 24 closes, 12h of relative volume, 1h/24h/7d returns, distance from the
7-day high, BTC and market breadth. The **coin name and date are hidden**, so it can't recall the outcome.
temperature 0, no thinking, ~3 s/call, 376 calls. Script: [llm-gate-2026-10-15/llm_gate.py](llm-gate-2026-10-15/llm_gate.py);
`results.csv` and `decisions.csv` (every question, the answer, and that trade's result) are next to it.
The model's answers are cached in `cache.json`.

## Is the model picking good trades? No, slightly worse than chance

Each signal the model judged, scored by what that trade alone would have returned after costs:

| Config | Model said | Signals | Avg trade | Win rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| loose | take | 89 | −0.25% | 18% |
| loose | skip | 215 | −0.19% | 21% |
| strict | take | 34 | −0.29% | 21% |
| strict | skip | 38 | −0.14% | 26% |

- **The trades the model approved did worse than the ones it rejected**, on both rule sets.
- Where the gated version beats rules-only (loose, short windows), the only reason is fewer trades.
  It saves fees ($69 vs $159 over 90d), not skill. Skipping trades at random would do about as well.
- The confidence score is effectively a yes/no flag. It's almost always 10–15 (skip) or 85 (take).
- Its approve pattern is "huge volume spike + at the 7-day high = institutional accumulation, continuation."
  In practice those are often blow-off tops, which the 1-bar exit then sells into.
  It rejects when the last hour was red, which is close to what the exit rule already does.

## Verdict
This LLM doesn't add an edge as a trade filter for this strategy. The strategy itself still loses at Alpaca fees
(see 2026-10-13). Possible retries: a stronger, less heavily quantized model, thinking on, or using the model to
*avoid* "volume spike at the high" setups. None of these fixes the fee drag on a 1-hour-hold strategy.
